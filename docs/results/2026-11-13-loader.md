# The home page's loader, 2026-11-13

What the loader's cover costs the GPU test and how long it keeps the page covered (phase P4.5, REQ-LOAD-01, REQ-TIER-01, REQ-PERF-01, REQ-PERF-04). Every figure below was measured on 13 Nov 2026 on the builds named here; nothing is estimated.

## Builds

- Round 1: build `3UwEobFoBh-4gLk2c6ab2`, the loader as first put together, with `data-loader-done` on the cover.
- Round 2: build `HGcCpcP7Kjo7kNKIK6QUy`, after two look fixes (the hole drawn black, a wave's flight 400 ms).
- Light round: build `8MYRlUMk9uZ4hVOjgskdY`, a one-off with `LIGHT_DURING_TEST` set to true, then set back to false.
- Budget: build `N8I1iyEEKYUNe3bLDDpyc`, round 2's source rebuilt after the light round, and build `WOhwDDSivbkC0X7KUF5UE`, the final source: round 2's with the ripple's wave on the mockup's cubic ease out. The ripple runs only after the lift has begun, so it changes none of the figures above the budget; the two budget tables were the same, line for line.

All four with Next.js 16.4.5, `pnpm build`, served by `next start` on port 3110.

## Machine

- This PC: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Ti, Windows 11 Pro. The display runs at 240 Hz: every requestAnimationFrame median below is 4.2 ms, one refresh, on every setup, so a median here cannot show a cost smaller than a refresh.
- Browser: Chromium 158.0.8127.18 (Playwright 1.65.0, channel chromium). Node v26.12.1.

## Method

Scratch Playwright scripts outside the repo drove the production build. Each run launched a fresh browser with a fresh profile, so every load was a first visit. Runs with and without the cover alternated, five of each per setup and round.

- Without the cover: an init script set session storage `portfolio:loader` to `seen` before any page script, so the inline script marked the page `data-loader-off="seen"`, as on a later home load in the tab.
- Tier 1: headed, 1280 x 800, the real GPU; the engine chose the tier itself (the probe).
- Tier 2 forced: headed, 1280 x 800, `navigator.gpu` hidden by an init script (the `asTier2` pattern in e2e/support/tiers.ts).
- Phone profile: the one REQ-PERF-01 uses (e2e/vitals.spec.ts): Pixel 5 emulation at 375 x 812 with touch, headless as the phone-vitals project runs, Chrome DevTools Protocol throttling at Slow 4G (latency 562.5 ms, 180,000 bytes a second down, 84,375 up) and a 4x CPU slowdown. The engine chose tier 2 with reason phone, as on a phone.
- Frame times: every requestAnimationFrame timestamp in the page, from an init script, as intervals.
- The GPU test's window: from probeStarted to tierChosen. probeStarted was found two ways: the cover's `data-loader-test` turning `running`, and in both modes a test-side wrapper of `setTimeout` that notes the moment the probe sets its 2,000 ms timer; the two agreed within 0.2 ms in every cover run. tierChosen is the field wrapper's `data-tier` being set. "Counted" frames are those after the probe's 500 ms warm-up (`PROBE.warmupMs`), the ones the probe judges.
- Where no probe runs (tier 2 by the decision table, and phones) there is no GPU test to change, so the table gives the 2 s after tierChosen instead: the field's first frames while the cover draws, its hold and the start of its exit.
- Times: from attribute changes seen by a MutationObserver. Engine loaded, renderer started, tier chosen and ready are `data-loader-done` reaching 1, 2, 3 and 4 (ready is also the field's `data-ready`); the lift began at `data-loader-state="leaving"`; the page was uncovered at the html element's `data-loader="done"`, set once the exit has ended and the page is free. Every time is in ms after the first contentful paint, the moment the cover's lift is timed from; FCP and LCP are in ms after navigation start.
- LCP: the vitals spec's observer (largest-contentful-paint, buffered, from an init script), read 2.5 s after the lift ended with the cover, or after the field was ready without it. The vitals spec itself was not run: its config starts its own server on port 3100.

Findings:

- The chosen tier never changed, and neither did the median frame time, on any setup in any round. By the phase's rule the light scene stays off: `LIGHT_DURING_TEST` is false. The light round was run anyway, to see what it would change; it is recorded above and changed neither figure.
- Tier 1 (round 2): in both modes the window opens inside one long frame of 192 to 200 ms, which began 65 to 70 ms before it. With the cover, two more follow inside the warm-up, 300 to 308 ms at 130 to 134 ms into the window and 112.5 to 116.7 ms at 431 to 439 ms, and the next frame, at 547 to 555 ms, is the one counted frame of 25.0 to 29.3 ms. Without the cover the window holds no frame over 4.4 ms. So on this PC the cover costs the probe 16 to 19 counted frames of about 360 and adds one long one; the median holds.
- Tier 2 forced: one frame of 104 to 121 ms at the moment the field reports ready, in 7 of the 10 cover runs (4 in round 1, 3 in round 2); without the cover the frame at ready is 20.8 to 25 ms. A Chrome trace of one cover run shows that frame as the main thread waiting on the GPU process, behind a raster flush, for 115 ms; without the cover the same step took 0.9 ms. The same wait is the likely cause of the extra stalls in the tier 1 window, not proven. Both modes also have one frame of 529 to 671 ms as the WebGL2 field is built.
- Phone profile: the cover doubles the 95th percentile (8.4 against 4.3 ms, two refreshes against one) while the field starts, holds and the exit begins, and 30 to 40 fewer frames arrive in those 2 s at the 4x CPU slowdown. The median holds. No probe runs on a phone, so no tier is at stake there.
- Not measured: a weaker desktop GPU, where the probe decides between tier 1 and tier 2 near its 20 ms line. On this PC every frame fits one 240 Hz refresh, so the margin to that line is about 15.8 ms.

- On both desktop setups the cover is taken over and its scene paints its first frame at about the first contentful paint (round 2 medians: 4 ms before it on tier 1, 178 ms before it on tier 2). On the phone profile the island takes the cover over 2,581 ms after the first contentful paint (when the first-load scripts have arrived and hydrated) and the scene paints 3,168 ms after it: until then the cover is the void with its label. The scene's own chunk arrives about 600 ms after the takeover there (one Slow 4G round trip; requested at 3,595 ms after navigation, received at 4,195 ms, in a resource timing run on round 1's build).
- The field is ready later with the cover on every setup (round 2 medians): tier 1 by 145 ms counted from the first contentful paint (82 ms from navigation start), tier 2 by 171 ms (153 ms), the phone profile by 119 ms (92 ms).
- The lift: tier 2 always lifts at the 2 s minimum (1,999.9 to 2,002.0 ms after the first contentful paint, read to 0.1 ms); tier 1 at ready plus the 700 ms hold; the phone profile at ready plus the hold, 7.8 s after the first paint. The exit then takes 1.9 s (the glide, the default) before the page is free.
- The time limit was not reached on any of these runs. With the engine's entry chunk held back so it never answers, the cover lifted at 10,001 ms after the first contentful paint with the outcome `limit` (one run, checks in the review folder).

## Proposals for T7SEN

- The time limit: keep `LIFT_LIMIT_MS` at 10,000 ms. The slowest setup measured, the phone profile, begins its lift 7.8 s after the first paint (7,798 to 7,837 ms in round 2), 2.2 s inside the limit. A limit of 8 s would leave it about 0.2 s, and any phone a little slower than the profile would lift onto a page whose field has not drawn. The figures do not show 10,000 ms is wrong.
- REQ-PERF-01, rewritten from these figures: "Readable content (LCP) in 2 s or less on a mid-range phone, on every route and on every later home load in a tab. On the first home load in a tab, the loader's cover paints with the first frame (first contentful paint in 2 s or less) and the hero is uncovered within the time limit (LIFT_LIMIT_MS, 10 s after the first paint) plus the exit (about 1.9 s); on the phone profile it was uncovered 9.7 s after the first paint. Chrome's LCP does not see the cover, so the vitals spec records the cover's lift and uncovered times beside LCP on that load."
